# Mirellis Service Blueprint V1

**Status:** product reset proposal for approval. This document authorises no app, site, content, research, or live deployment change.  
**Date:** 12 August 2026  
**Governing sources:** App Reset — V1 Product Brief; V1 canonical-transition model; Personal Observatory trust boundary; curriculum stewardship V3; Launch & Learning Operations.

## Executive decision

Mirellis should not try to be the world’s biggest wellness-content library, a diagnosis engine, or a generic habit app. Its credible product job is narrower and more valuable:

> Help a person who has explicitly named a difficult transition make one useful, practical next move—and, when useful, give them the right supporting environment around that move.

The first release must therefore be **one complete Work → Recovery service**, not twelve thin routes across four transitions. The current broad route prototype is useful only as an internal learning artefact; it is not a release candidate.

The consumer should encounter a **transition session**, not a sequence of app screens:

```text
Recognition
→ a real first move
→ a quiet, timed transition environment
→ optional sound / physical ritual / film / reading at the appropriate point
→ an honest close
→ a private record only if the person chooses it
```

The entire service works without a purchase, account, or research participation. Products, Findings, films, articles, and social channels are supporting instruments—not prerequisites or detours.

## 1. What the product is and is not

### It is

- A private, local-first companion for ordinary, non-clinical transition moments.
- A clear route from a person’s own chosen description to a practical action.
- A way to bring Mirellis sound, rituals, films, Findings, and products into one coherent experience when each has a specific job.
- A deliberately small experience that preserves interruption, uncertainty, and leaving without penalty.

### It is not

- A medical, therapeutic, crisis, sleep-treatment, or diagnosis product.
- A dashboard of readiness, recovery, productivity, or self-improvement.
- A content feed, course catalogue, or generic quiz system.
- An automated interpretation engine: it does not infer state, parse free-text notes, or claim to know what a person needs.
- A funnel that withholds a useful transition until someone watches, subscribes, purchases, or shares data.

## 2. The strategic product architecture

The app must make the existing Mirellis ecosystem legible without asking a person to navigate it at the worst moment.

| Layer | Job | Default position in the journey | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recognition | Let the person recognise the moment in plain language. | First | “Work is still with me.” |
| Transition session | Deliver one small, practical action and a contained environment for doing it. | Core | Closing a work surface; changing a room cue; 8–10-minute Stillness session. |
| Support asset | Deepen or sustain the selected session only where it helps. | Inside or after the action | Stillness playlist, a short Mirellis film, a ritual cue. |
| Understanding | Explain the relevant Observatory context, without turning it into a promise. | After the session, optional | A reviewed Work → Recovery Finding. |
| Instrument | Offer a physical ritual tool as optional continuity. | After usefulness is established, optional | Stillness Elixir and Formula Dossier. |
| Personal Observatory | Keep only deliberate, local personal evidence. | After completion or deliberate saving | Chosen route, chosen outcome, optional authored note. |
| Research participation | Invite contribution only through separate, plain consent. | Never in the core session | Separate, optional future programme. |

### The key distinction

Mirellis can be richer than a meditation app without becoming an overwhelming library. The system does not say, “Here are 50 things to try.” It says, “For the moment you named, start here. This is optional support if you want it. This is why it belongs here.”

## 3. Canonical scope

### Consumer-active V1 pilot

**One transition:** After work / Deep Focus → Recovery.  
**One session format:** 8–10 minutes.  
**Four explicit entry situations:**

1. “An unfinished point is still replaying.”
2. “I keep checking and cannot disengage.”
3. “My body still feels switched on.”
4. “I am not sure; I just have not switched off.”

These are not diagnoses. They are person-selected descriptions that determine the first practical cue. If a choice does not produce a materially different first cue, it must be removed.

### Canonical transition model (retained, not expanded yet)

The full V1 curriculum contains four canonical transitions:

1. Sleep → Wakefulness
2. Wakefulness → Readiness
3. Readiness → Deep Focus
4. Deep Focus → Recovery

They remain the future expansion frame. They are **not** a reason to expose four incomplete products at launch.

“Stimulation → Restoration” remains a website/editorial and sound collection until it has its own approved product model; it must not be presented as an active fifth app transition.

## 4. The flagship service: After Work

### The user promise

“In about ten minutes, you will mark the end of one work period, make one chosen shift in your environment, and decide what comes next. Mirellis does not promise a particular feeling.”

### Session anatomy

| Moment | Purpose | What the person does | What Mirellis does | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Recognition | Start from a lived situation, not a curriculum label. | Chooses the closest of four statements. | Acknowledges only the selected statement and says what the session will help them do. | 15–30 sec |
| 2. First cue | Make an immediate, observable change before any content consumption. | Completes the entry-specific cue. | Shows one instruction, not an explanation. | 1–2 min |
| 3. Closure environment | Create a real boundary around the end of work. | Closes/puts away a work surface; changes light, room, or device position. | Begins a chosen Stillness listening environment only when the person taps to open it. | 1–2 min |
| 4. Descent | Give the person a quiet, non-demanding stretch outside the app. | Walks, sits, showers, makes food, or undertakes one chosen ordinary recovery action. | Offers one clear optional ritual cue and a visible “return when useful” state. | 4–6 min |
| 5. Honest close | Let the person describe what is true without being graded. | Chooses: a little more room / partly enough / not for me today / nothing yet. | Stores only an explicit outcome when the person chooses to keep it. | 20–40 sec |
| 6. Optional continuity | Give a useful next door, not a sales pressure. | May save the exact route, open the Finding, use a ritual tool, or return to today. | Explains each option’s exact effect. | Optional |

### Materially different first cues

| Selected entry | First cue | Why this differs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unfinished point replaying | Write the next visible action in one plain line, then close or put away one work surface. | Creates a return point before the person leaves work. |
| Checking and cannot disengage | Make one chosen cue less available, then decide whether a later check is genuinely needed. | Creates distance from the repeated trigger without pretending urgency does not exist. |
| Body still switched on | Find physical support, then make one safe sensory or environmental change. | Begins with the body/environment rather than task closure. |
| Not sure / not switched off | Look around, name one ordinary next place for attention, and move there. | Avoids forcing an explanation when the person does not have one. |

### Support-media rules

- **Stillness sound:** is the core ambient support. It must be an approved public playlist, described accurately as opening in Spotify. Mirellis must not claim it was heard.
- **Film:** a 3–5 minute film is not a substitute for the session. It is shown only before the person begins if they want context, or after the session if they want to understand why Mirellis studies this transition. The current YouTube inventory does not contain a purpose-built After Work film; no video control appears until one exists.
- **Finding/article:** appears after the transition under “Understand this moment.” It must use reviewed, qualified language and must not promise the practice will produce the described effect.
- **Stillness Elixir:** appears only after the person has completed or deliberately saved a practice, framed as “A ritual tool for this transition.” It is never a requirement or treatment claim.
- **No quiz, game, course, or form** is included simply to create activity. A structured prompt belongs only if it changes the first practical cue or helps the person make a decision they could not otherwise make.

## 5. Screen and interaction contract

### First-time route

```text
Today
→ “Work is still with me”
→ four specific descriptions
→ why this practice / start this practice
→ first cue
→ closure environment + optional Stillness
→ leave to do the real-world action
→ check-in
→ clear close and optional doors
```

### Required screen principles

- The person reaches the first real-world action within two decisions.
- The app does not simulate watching/listening/completing. External actions are external actions.
- One primary action and one honest exit per screen.
- “Leave for now” preserves the visible resume point but does not create a completion, outcome, or route.
- “Keep this practice for tomorrow” is shown only after a completed practice, and immediately changes Today to “Return to this practice.”
- “Choose another approach” goes to the other three After Work entry situations, not back to an undifferentiated start.
- Personal Observatory begins empty and contains only a completed or deliberately saved route plus the person’s optional note.

### Copy contract

| Never say | Use instead |
| --- | --- |
| “You have recovered.” | “What is true now, if anything?” |
| “Your nervous system needs…” | “You chose that your body still feels switched on.” |
| “Continue” for an untouched route | “Start this practice.” |
| “Keep this experience” | “Keep this practice for tomorrow,” with visible consequence. |
| “Your progress” | “Your private record.” |
| “Recommended for you” from hidden signals | “You saved this practice before.” |

## 6. Asset operating system

No channel is a route by itself. Every asset must have a route role before it is made or published.

### Required asset record

```ts
type RouteAsset = {
  id: string;
  title: string;
  kind: 'sound' | 'film' | 'finding' | 'article' | 'ritual-tool' | 'social-distribution';
  routeId: string;
  moment: 'before' | 'during' | 'after';
  job: string; // one plain-language sentence
  publicUrl: string;
  duration?: string;
  owner: string;
  rightsStatus: 'confirmed' | 'pending';
  claimStatus: 'reviewed' | 'editorial-context' | 'not-app-eligible';
  lastCheckedAt: string;
};
```

An asset with a blank URL, uncertain rights, unreviewed claim, or unclear route job does not render in the app.

## 7. Content system beyond the pilot

After the pilot, build a **route library**, not a content library. The following is a discovery backlog, not consumer-active app content.

| Future destination | Candidate lived situations to research and validate | Likely instruments |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start the morning | Awake but not present; phone capture; rushed start; waking into worry; not sure how to begin. | Morning Clarity, first-light ritual, short waking film, reviewed Finding. |
| Get going | Day feels too large; inbound messages take over; cannot decide first task; avoiding a start; low confidence in a beginning. | Small-horizon practice, Morning Clarity, start-of-day film/article. |
| Focus on one thing | Too many competing tasks; repeated interruption; cannot return after interruption; task feels too ambiguous; attention already depleted. | Flow, focus-window practice, return cue, reviewed Finding. |
| After work | Open loop; checking; activated body; unmarked ending; evening already consumed by another demand. | Stillness, closure cue, ritual tool, After Work film/Finding. |

Each candidate must earn inclusion through observed user language, a specific practical route, an available or commissioned asset, safety/editorial review, and pilot evidence. It does not become a consumer option merely because it sounds plausible.

## 8. Trust, privacy, and safety boundaries

- All practice data remains local-first and account-free for the pilot.
- Private note text is never read by recommendation logic, analytics, content selection, or research.
- The product stores only explicit selections, explicit outcome, optional note, and explicit save. It stores no inferred effectiveness or personality model.
- No automatic research donation. Any future participation uses a separate consent explanation, selectable scope, and withdrawal/deletion path.
- The app includes a clear boundary that it is for ordinary moments and does not provide emergency, clinical, or crisis support.
- Every interactive target is at least 44 × 44 px; focus is visible; routes work by keyboard; status messages are announced; motion respects reduced-motion preferences.

## 9. What must be tested before any expansion

### Pilot method

Run a small consent-respecting qualitative pilot with 8–12 people who regularly experience an after-work boundary problem. The purpose is to learn whether the service is understandable and useful, not to maximise engagement.

Test each entry situation, interruption/resume, leaving early, optional sound handoff, optional note, explicit save, Personal Observatory, Settings, deletion cancel/confirm, keyboard-only navigation, and mobile use.

### Questions to answer

1. Did the person understand what Mirellis was offering within 30 seconds?
2. Did they reach a real-world action without feeling instructed through a course?
3. Did the selected entry description feel true enough to choose?
4. Was the sound handoff useful and correctly timed—or distracting?
5. Did they understand that a product, Finding, and private record were optional?
6. Did any language imply diagnosis, pressure, or a promised result?
7. When it did not fit, could they leave or choose another approach without confusion?

### Launch gates

Do not move beyond the pilot until:

- Participants can explain the product job in their own words.
- Every one of the four entry choices produces a visibly distinct first cue.
- No essential step requires music, video, a product, account, or data sharing.
- The complete service can be used calmly on a phone and by keyboard.
- The public playlist, reading, and product language have approved ownership, rights, links, and claim status.
- Named product, editorial, privacy, accessibility, support, incident, and rollback owners sign the release record.

## 10. Delivery order

### Phase A — service specification and content readiness

1. Approve this blueprint and the private local-first pilot boundary.
2. Confirm Stillness playlist ownership and public URL.
3. Approve the exact four entry cues, session timing, final check-in copy, and product door.
4. Review one Finding for post-session use and commission Film 01.

### Phase B — single vertical-slice prototype

1. Remove the current broad 12-route consumer experience from the release path.
2. Build one complete After Work session using approved real assets only.
3. Test all state, accessibility, privacy, and interruption paths in a development environment.
4. Run the qualitative pilot; record only consented feedback outside the participant’s private app data.

### Phase C — deliberate expansion

1. Revise the After Work service from pilot evidence.
2. Produce and validate the next transition’s assets before its app route is built.
3. Add one transition at a time; never surface incomplete destinations as “coming soon.”

## Decisions required before Phase B

1. Confirm that Mirellis V1 is a **private local practice companion**, not an Observatory-connected learning instrument.
2. Confirm the approved public Stillness playlist URL and ownership/usage rights.
3. Decide whether Stillness Elixir appears after a completed practice in the pilot, or is deferred until after the qualitative pilot.
4. Approve the Film 01 brief and name its content owner.
5. Name the human approvers for editorial claims, accessibility, privacy, support/incident response, and publication.

Until these are decided, the only responsible work is specification, content readiness, and prototype preparation—not another live deployment.
